Sleep Cycle alternative: Sleep Cycle vs Alarm Clock Planner

Sleep Cycle is a sleep tracker that also wakes you up. If what you mostly need is the alarm, you may not need the tracker at all: an Apple Watch worn to bed already records your sleep in the Health app.*

Pick Sleep Cycle if you want sleep analysis from your phone on the nightstand, snore detection, sleep sounds, or a smart alarm that wakes you in a lighter sleep phase, and you are fine paying after a trial and signing up for an account.
Pick Alarm Clock Planner if you mostly want reliable alarms, already track sleep with an Apple Watch, or would rather not have an app listen to you sleep.

Where Sleep Cycle is stronger

Sleep analysis without a watch

Sleep stages, trends, snore and cough detection and an AI sleep coach, worked out from what the phone's microphone hears on the nightstand through the night. Setup will not go on without that microphone access.

A smart wake-up window

Its Smart Alarm aims for a lighter sleep phase within a window you choose, 30 minutes by default. Read the other way, that is an alarm that can go off up to 30 minutes before the time you set, on the strength of what the phone heard overnight. It covers one wake-up time, and you start it each night by pressing Start at bedtime.

Sleep sounds

Soundscapes and sleep stories to fall asleep to.

Where Alarm Clock Planner is stronger

Your sleep data stays with Apple Health

If you wear an Apple Watch to bed, it already records your sleep stages in the Health app, which keeps them on your devices and encrypts them end to end when synced through iCloud. No microphone listening all night, no account. Alarm Clock Planner just handles the alarms.

Alarms for plans, not just mornings

The early flight next Thursday, the school run on weekdays, a nap after lunch: any date on a calendar, monthly and yearly repeats, tags you switch on and off together, and one-tap naps. Sleep Cycle is built around one wake-up time, started each night.

Free to start, nothing to sign up for

Up to 3 alarms are free, with no trial, no account and 0 ads. When we installed Sleep Cycle, its setup ended on a screen whose only way on was a free trial, and once the trial started it wanted an account before going any further.

No tracking

Alarm Clock Planner's App Store privacy label lists no data used to track you or linked to you. Sleep Cycle's lists both.

What Alarm Clock Planner does not do

  • Track your sleep. An Apple Watch does that into the Health app, if you want it. Alarm Clock Planner sets alarms and rings them.
  • Use the microphone. Nothing listens to you sleep.
  • Start you on a trial or ask for an account. Download it and set an alarm. Unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once, when you want them.
  • 0 ads, even in the free version.

Pricing

Sleep Cycle is free to download, but its setup ends on a free trial: 7 days, then a yearly plan of about $30 to $35. Alarm Clock Planner's free version has 0 ads and no trial, and holds up to 3 alarms at a time; unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Questions

Do I need a sleep tracking app to wake up on time?

No. An alarm app is enough to wake up. If you also want sleep data, an Apple Watch worn to bed records it in the Health app once sleep tracking is turned on.

Does Sleep Cycle use Apple's native alarms?

Yes, it asks for them on iOS 26. Its Smart Alarm still has to be started in the app each night.

Does Alarm Clock Planner track my sleep?

No. It sets and rings alarms, and its App Store privacy label lists no data linked to you.
