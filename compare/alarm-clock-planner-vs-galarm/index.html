Galarm alternative: Galarm vs Alarm Clock Planner

Galarm is the closest app to Alarm Clock Planner: both set alarms for any date and handle repeats well beyond weekdays. They part ways on sharing, accounts and how the alarm actually rings.*

Pick Galarm if you want alarms that involve other people, like one group alarm for the whole family, and you are happy for them to arrive as notifications, or if your iPhone cannot update to iOS 26.
Pick Alarm Clock Planner if you want alarms that ring like the Clock app, on any date you pick, with no account, no ads and a lower price.

Where Galarm is stronger

Waking more than one person

Galarm is built for alarms that involve other people. A group alarm goes off on everyone's phone before the early flight, a buddy alarm lets you set one on your partner's phone, and every alarm has its own chat and confirmations. Everyone needs Galarm installed, and since shared alarms arrive as notifications rather than native alarms, each phone also needs Galarm's notifications, ideally Critical Alerts too, switched on. If someone skipped that during setup, their alarm may not sound.

Every hour, in any time zone

It can repeat down to every hour, handy for medication or a glass of water, and its alarms follow you when you fly to another time zone.

Older iPhones

Galarm runs on iOS 15.6 and later, so it still works on iPhones that cannot update to iOS 26: the iPhone XS, XS Max and XR from 2018, and anything older. Every iPhone from the iPhone 11 (2019) onward runs it, and in June 2026 Apple counted 79% of all iPhones in use already on iOS 26, before iOS 27 came out. Native alarms for apps only arrived with iOS 26, which is why Galarm still rings through notifications.

Where Alarm Clock Planner is stronger

It rings like the Clock app

An alarm only has one job. Alarm Clock Planner uses Apple's native alarms, which iOS 26 opened to apps, so it rings full screen through Silent, Focus and a locked phone, the same way Apple does it with their Clock app. Galarm alerts through notifications, asks for Critical Alerts (Apple's channel for urgent health and safety warnings) to get past the Silent switch, and some reviews say its alarms stayed quiet, or gave a few short beeps, on Silent.

Made for a long list

Work, school runs and the odd trip each get a tag. A tag can have its own sound and snooze length, switches off in one tap for a holiday, and the month calendar shows every alarm at a glance.

Less to pay

Unlimited alarms are $9.99 once, or $2.99 a year. Galarm Premium is $9.99 a year or $24.99 once.

What we saw using Galarm

From 20 to 23 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • It asked to send Critical Alerts, which sound even when the iPhone is muted. We said no, and its main screen showed 'Galarm doesn't have the required permissions to ring alarms. Tap to fix.'
  • Tapping the notice opened Galarm's notification settings in iOS Settings, a page of banners, badges and sounds with nothing pointing to the switch it needed. We backed out, and tapping the notice again did not bring us back, so our alarms stayed unable to ring.
  • Its alarm sound defaults to 'Critical, 80% volume'.
  • It does not use Apple's native alarms, so every alarm is a notification rather than a real iPhone alarm.

What Alarm Clock Planner does not do

  • Share alarms or chat. To wake a group at the same time, everyone can simply set the alarm on their own phone, in Alarm Clock Planner (free for up to 3 alarms) or in the Clock app. With Galarm, everyone has to install it, join the group and get its notification settings right, and each of those steps is a chance for someone to sleep through.
  • Ask for your phone number, your contacts or an account. Galarm sends your phone number to a verification service, uploads your address book to find your friends and keeps your alarms on its servers (its privacy policy, version 2025.05.12). Alarm Clock Planner doesn't share or upload your alarms or personal details: they stay on your device and in your own iCloud backup.
  • 0 ads, even in the free version.
  • Ring through notification workarounds. Every alarm is a native iPhone alarm, so there is no Critical Alerts permission or notification setting to get right before it can wake you.

Alarm Clock Planner's free version has no ads at all and holds up to 3 alarms at a time; unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Questions

Can both apps set alarms for a specific date?

Yes, both let you pick any future date and repeat monthly or yearly. The difference shows on the day. Alarm Clock Planner hands the alarm to the iPhone as a native alarm, so it rings reliably. Galarm sends a notification, which only sounds if its notification settings, and on mute its Critical Alerts, are still switched on, so an alarm planned weeks ahead has more that can quietly go wrong.

Do I need an account?

Galarm signs you in with your phone number and keeps your alarms on its servers. Alarm Clock Planner needs no account; alarms stay on your phone and in your own iCloud backup.

Which one can share alarms?

Galarm. Everyone involved needs Galarm installed with its notifications set up, since a shared alarm arrives as a notification, not a native alarm. Alarm Clock Planner does not share alarms.
