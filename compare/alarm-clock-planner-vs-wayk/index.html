Wayk alternative: Wayk vs Alarm Clock Planner

Wayk makes you get up and do something, push-ups or a photo of the sky, before the alarm stops. That works for some people. Others find a task at 6 AM is one more thing to fight, and do better fixing the wake-up schedule itself.*

Pick Wayk if a task you must finish is the only thing that gets you out of bed, and you are happy to pay a subscription for it.
Pick Alarm Clock Planner if you want an alarm that is easy to stop, backup alarms a few minutes apart, a snooze length that suits you, and no subscription.

Where Wayk is stronger

Getting you out of bed

A mission you have to complete is hard to sleep through, which is the whole point for heavy sleepers.

A morning routine

Missions like making your bed or reading an affirmation can start the day with a habit.

Where Alarm Clock Planner is stronger

Easy to stop, easy to plan

Alarms ring as system alarms with Stop and Snooze on the lock screen. Instead of a puzzle, set two or three alarms a few minutes apart in one group and switch them on or off together.

Snooze and naps your way

Snooze from 1 to 30 minutes, set per group, and one-tap naps of 10 to 30 minutes, so you can find a wake-up schedule that works.

No subscription needed

Up to 3 alarms free, then $2.99 a year or $9.99 once. Wayk's listing says a subscription is needed to set alarms, with plans listed from $6.99 to $59.99.

No tracking

Alarm Clock Planner's privacy label lists no data used to track you. Wayk's lists data used to track you.

About Alarm Clock Planner

An iPhone alarm clock built around the calendar, for alarms you plan ahead.

What we saw using Wayk

On 20 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • Setup is about 20 question screens, then asks you to sign a 'commitment' before the paywall.
  • Its setup promises 'No more snoozing, backup alarms, or scrolling in bed'.
  • Alarms need a subscription; we started the free trial to see the rest, and cancelled it.
  • After the trial it asked for the microphone, to detect snoring and coughing, and for the camera.
  • With the app closed, the alarm rang as the iOS system alarm.

Alarm Clock Planner, same phone, same day:

  • With the app closed, the alarm rang as the iOS system alarm, with snooze and stop on the banner, the same as the Clock app.
  • Its upgrade screen, on the same EU store: €2.29 a year or €7.79 once, or carry on free with up to 3 alarms. No ads, no account.

What Alarm Clock Planner does not do

  • No wake-up missions or sleep tracking
  • No shared alarms

What recent reviews of Wayk mention

From US App Store reviews of Wayk read on 20 October 2026 (most recent and most helpful). These are individual experiences, not tests we ran, but these themes came up more than once:

  • having to start a paid plan before setting any alarm, after a long setup
  • a free trial that turned into a paid year
  • missions or object scanning that glitch or misread
  • missions that were not strict enough to keep them up

Pricing

Wayk is free to download; its listing says a subscription is needed to set alarms, with plans listed from $6.99 to $59.99 (US). Alarm Clock Planner is free for up to 3 alarms; unlimited alarms cost $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Questions

Do wake-up missions actually help?

For some people, yes. If you find yourself fighting the task half asleep, a steadier schedule often works better: backup alarms a few minutes apart, a snooze length you choose, and naps you set in one tap.

Can I use Wayk for free?

Its listing says an active subscription is required to set alarms after the setup questions.

Try Alarm Clock Planner

Free for up to 3 alarms. Rings on Silent. No account needed.
