Wayk alternative: Wayk vs Alarm Clock Planner

Wayk makes you get up and do something, push-ups or a photo of the sky, before the alarm stops. That works for some people. Others find a task at 6 AM is one more thing to fight, and do better fixing the wake-up schedule itself.*

Pick Wayk if a task you must finish is the only thing that gets you out of bed, and you are happy to pay a subscription for it.
Pick Alarm Clock Planner if you want an alarm that is easy to stop, backup alarms a few minutes apart, a snooze length that suits you, and no subscription.

Where Wayk is stronger

Getting you out of bed

Push-ups, a photo of the sky, making your bed: a mission you have to finish is hard to sleep through, which is the whole point for heavy sleepers. It comes at a price, though: alarms need a subscription, after a setup of about 20 questions and a signed 'commitment'. Before paying, ask whether you need a task or just a second chance. If it is the second, a backup alarm a few minutes later, or a snooze limit you set, does the same job without the push-ups.

A morning routine

Missions like making your bed or reading an affirmation can start the day with a habit.

Where Alarm Clock Planner is stronger

Easy to stop, easy to plan

Alarms ring full screen with Snooze and a slide to stop, the same way Apple does it with their Clock app. Instead of a task, set two or three alarms a few minutes apart in one tag and switch them on or off together. Wayk's own setup promises 'No more snoozing, backup alarms, or scrolling in bed'; backup alarms are fine, as long as they are easy to manage.

Snooze and naps your way

Snooze from 1 to 30 minutes, set per tag, a limit of 1, 2 or 3 snoozes so the last one really is the last, and one-tap naps of 10 to 30 minutes, so you can find a wake-up schedule that works.

No subscription needed

Up to 3 alarms are free, and unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once. Wayk needs a subscription before you can set your first alarm, with plans listed from $6.99 to $59.99 (US).

No tracking

Alarm Clock Planner's privacy label lists no data used to track you. Wayk's lists data used to track you.

What we saw using Wayk

From 20 to 23 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • Setup is about 20 question screens, ending with a 'commitment' to sign.
  • Then a paywall: alarms need a subscription. We started the free trial to see the rest, and cancelled it.
  • Along the way it promises 'No more snoozing, backup alarms, or scrolling in bed'.
  • Once the trial started, it asked for the microphone, to detect snoring and coughing, and for the camera.
  • With the app closed, the alarm went off as a native alarm, as it should.

What Alarm Clock Planner does not do

  • Make you do push-ups. Stopping is a slide. If a task is truly the only thing that gets you up, Wayk is built for that.
  • Ask for a subscription before your first alarm. Set up to 3 alarms free, with no trial to cancel.
  • Ask for your camera or microphone. Wayk asked for both once its trial started: the microphone to detect snoring and coughing, and the camera, which its photo missions use.

What recent reviews of Wayk mention

From US App Store reviews of Wayk read on 23 October 2026 (most recent and most helpful). These are individual experiences, not tests we ran, but these themes came up more than once:

  • having to start a paid plan before setting any alarm, after a long setup
  • an annual plan bought within days, with no refund
  • missions or object scanning that glitch or misread
  • missions that are easy to cheat

Pricing

Wayk is free to download, but alarms need a subscription: plans listed from $6.99 to $59.99 (US), starting with a free trial. Alarm Clock Planner's free version holds up to 3 alarms at a time, with no trial to cancel; unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Questions

Do wake-up missions actually help?

For some people, yes. If you find yourself fighting the task half asleep, a steadier schedule often works better: backup alarms a few minutes apart, a snooze length you choose, and naps you set in one tap.

Can I use Wayk for free?

Its listing says an active subscription is required to set alarms after the setup questions.
