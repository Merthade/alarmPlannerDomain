Alarm Clock for Me alternative: Alarm Clock for Me vs Alarm Clock Planner

Alarm Clock for Me is a good-looking bedside clock. As an alarm it is weaker than it looks: the alarm is a notification, which asked us to turn Silent mode off before it could ring, and its paid plans are weekly or yearly subscriptions.*

Pick Alarm Clock for Me if you want designer clock faces on the nightstand and weather, and you are fine with an alarm that arrives as a notification and asks for Silent mode off.
Pick Alarm Clock Planner if you want alarms that ring as native alarms with Silent on, 0 ads and a one-time price.

Where Alarm Clock for Me is stronger

Nightstand clock faces

Designer clock faces, weather, a sleep timer and a flashlight, for a phone that sits on the nightstand all night. The iPhone's own StandBy mode already shows a big clock, the weather and widgets while it charges on its side, with no ads and nothing to buy.

Stop by shaking or solving

Shake the phone or solve a math problem to turn the alarm off. With the app closed, though, the alarm is a notification, so it follows the Silent switch.

Where Alarm Clock Planner is stronger

Rings with Silent on

Alarm Clock Planner uses Apple's native alarms, so it rings full screen through Silent and Focus with the app closed, the same way Apple does it with their Clock app. Alarm Clock for Me's alarm is a notification: in our test it asked us to switch Silent mode off so the alarm could ring, and its listing says it will not ring with notifications turned off.

Opens on your alarms, not a sales pitch

No upgrade screen on launch and 0 ads. Alarm Clock for Me opened on its upgrade screen every time we launched it, carries a banner ad, and covered the app with a full-screen ad after we saved an alarm.

Pay once

Unlimited alarms are $9.99 once or $2.99 a year. Alarm Clock for Me sells weekly plans from $0.99 to $2.99, or $19.99 a year (US).

Dates and tags

Alarms for any date, monthly and yearly repeats, and tags you switch on or off together.

What we saw using Alarm Clock for Me

On 20 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • It opened on its upgrade screen every time we launched it, offering weekly or yearly plans with no one-time option.
  • After we saved an alarm, a full-screen ad covered the app, and 40 seconds later we still had not found a way to close it.
  • When the alarm came, it was a notification asking us to turn Silent mode off so the alarm could ring.

What Alarm Clock Planner does not do

  • Make you shake or solve. Stopping is a slide, like the Clock app. For a second chance, set backup alarms a few minutes apart, or one alarm with your own snooze length and snooze limit.
  • Ask you to turn Silent off. Native alarms ring through it.
  • Charge by the week. Up to 3 alarms are free, then $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.
  • 0 ads, even in the free version.

Pricing

Alarm Clock for Me's free version comes with ads, banner and full-screen ones in our test, and it opens on its upgrade screen every launch. Premium costs $0.99 to $2.99 a week or $19.99 a year (US). Alarm Clock Planner's free version has 0 ads and holds up to 3 alarms at a time; unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Questions

Does Alarm Clock for Me need notifications to ring?

Yes. Its listing says the alarm will not ring with notifications turned off, and in our test its notification asked us to turn Silent mode off for the alarm to ring.

Is there a one-time purchase?

Not on Alarm Clock for Me's US purchase list, which shows weekly and yearly plans. Alarm Clock Planner has a $9.99 one-time unlock.
