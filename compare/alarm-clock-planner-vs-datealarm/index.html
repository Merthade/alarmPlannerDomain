DateAlarm alternative: DateAlarm vs Alarm Clock Planner

Both apps exist because the Clock app cannot set an alarm for a date. DateAlarm frames it around countdowns to birthdays and anniversaries. The catch is in how it rings: the native iPhone alarm is a paid membership feature, and the free version alerts with notifications.*

Pick DateAlarm if you mostly track birthdays and anniversaries with countdowns and notes, and you are happy to pay for membership to get a native alarm, or your iPhone cannot update to iOS 26.
Pick Alarm Clock Planner if you want a date alarm that rings like the Clock app on the free version too, with richer repeats and tags, for $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Where DateAlarm is stronger

Countdowns and categories

Countdowns to birthdays, anniversaries and deadlines, sorted into categories with a note on each, and a clean calendar to pick the date. The countdown is a nice touch, but what reaches you on the day is the alarm, and on the free version that is a notification.

Older iPhones

DateAlarm runs on iOS 15 and later, so it works on iPhones that cannot update to iOS 26: the iPhone XS, XS Max and XR from 2018, and anything older. Every iPhone from the iPhone 11 (2019) onward runs it, and in June 2026 Apple counted 79% of all iPhones in use already on iOS 26, before iOS 27 came out. On those older phones it can only alert through notifications, since native alarms for apps arrived with iOS 26.

Where Alarm Clock Planner is stronger

A real alarm without paying

Every Alarm Clock Planner alarm is a native iPhone alarm, on the free version too, so it rings through Silent and Focus with the app closed. DateAlarm keeps its 'Default iOS System Alarm' behind membership; its free version sends notifications, and warns that alarms cannot ring with notifications switched off.

Repeats and tags for a long list

Monthly, yearly and every-other-week repeats with end dates, tags you switch on and off together, and a month calendar of every alarm.

Less to pay

Unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once. DateAlarm's membership is $3.99 a week, $4.99 a month, $29.99 a year or $69.99 once (US).

What we saw using DateAlarm

From 20 to 23 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • It opened as a trial with 3 days left.
  • Without membership it alerts through notifications. With notifications off, it warns 'Alarms cannot ring when notifications are disabled'.
  • Its membership screen lists 'Default iOS System Alarm' among the paid features, next to weekly, yearly and lifetime plans.

What Alarm Clock Planner does not do

  • Count down to dates. Alarms show their date and time, not a countdown. If counting down to an anniversary is the point, DateAlarm does that.
  • Hold the native alarm back for paying users. Free or paid, every alarm rings as a native alarm.
  • Start you on a trial. There is nothing that runs out after 3 days. Up to 3 alarms are free, then $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Pricing

DateAlarm is free to download and opens as a 3-day trial; its membership costs $3.99 a week, $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year, or $69.99 once (US). Alarm Clock Planner's free version has no time limit, rings as a native alarm and holds up to 3 alarms at a time; unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Questions

Do both apps set alarms for a future date?

Yes, that is the core of both. The difference is how the alarm reaches you on the day: free Alarm Clock Planner alarms are native alarms, while DateAlarm's free version sends notifications, which stay silent if notifications are off.

Will the alarm ring on Silent?

Alarm Clock Planner's alarms ring through Silent and Focus, free or paid. DateAlarm lists the native iOS alarm as a membership feature; its free version uses notifications.
