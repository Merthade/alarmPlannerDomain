Alarmy alternative: Alarmy vs Alarm Clock Planner

These two apps solve different problems. Alarmy makes sure you get out of bed. Alarm Clock Planner makes sure the right alarm is waiting on the right day. Which one you want depends on which of those is your problem.*

Pick Alarmy if you truly sleep through ordinary alarms and need a task, like a math problem or a photo, before the alarm will stop, and you can live with ads or a subscription.
Pick Alarm Clock Planner if you want an alarm that is easy to stop, backup alarms or your own snooze pattern instead of a puzzle, alarms planned by date, and 0 ads, for $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Where Alarmy is stronger

Waking heavy sleepers

Missions are Alarmy's whole design: solve math, shake the phone, do squats or photograph a spot in another room before the alarm stops, with options to block snoozing and switching the phone off. If nothing else gets you up, that works. The catch is the same thing that makes it work: at 6 AM you are solving a puzzle half asleep, and reviewers describe a heavy push to buy the subscription on top.

Sleep features

A sleep tracker, snore detection, sleep sounds and bedtime reminders, all in one app. Snore detection means the microphone listening through the night, and the photo mission needs the camera. If you have an Apple Watch, you may not need any of it: the watch tracks your sleep stages into the Health app on its own, with no extra app and nothing more to pay.

Reach

32 languages, about 250,000 US ratings, and iPhones back to iOS 17. A star rating mostly says how the first days felt, not how the alarm holds up after a month: Alarmy asked us for one on our first day, before it had woken us a single morning. Popular is not the same as right for how you wake up. And on iPhones that cannot run iOS 26 (the iPhone XS, XS Max and XR from 2018, and anything older), no app can use Apple's native alarms.

Where Alarm Clock Planner is stronger

One alarm, one stop

A native alarm rings, you stop it, and that is the end of it, the same way Apple does it with their Clock app. Alarmy does ring as a native alarm first, but when we dismissed it, Alarmy's own page started ringing again: a dancing cat under the alarm's name, with an ad above and below. Dismissing that brought a full-screen ad. Two rings and an ad before you are out of bed. Funny cats on the first morning; by the tenth they are one more gimmick between you and the stop button.

Easy to stop, hard to sleep through

Instead of a puzzle, set two or three alarms a few minutes apart in one tag and switch them on or off together, or keep one alarm and give it your own snooze pattern: a snooze length from 1 to 30 minutes and a limit of 1, 2 or 3 snoozes, so the last one is the one that gets you up. Each rings full screen as a native alarm, with Snooze and a slide to stop and nothing else around it.

Alarms on dates, not just weekdays

Pick any day on a calendar, with monthly, yearly and every-other-week repeats and end dates. Alarmy's editor only repeats on days of the week, like the Clock app's; there is no date to pick.

0 ads at 6 AM

At 6 AM you would barely notice ads above and below your alarm. They are still being shown and paid for, though: in Alarmy's free version, the moment you wake up is part of what is being sold, and its privacy label lists data used to track you. Alarm Clock Planner has 0 ads, even in the free version.

Less to pay

Unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once. Alarmy's yearly plan is $59.99 on the US list, 20 times ours, and its lifetime plan is about $144, 14 times ours.

What we saw using Alarmy

From 20 to 23 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • With the app closed, the alarm first rang as Apple's native alarm, like the Clock app's.
  • When we dismissed it, Alarmy's own page took over and started ringing again: a dancing cat under the alarm's name, Snooze and Dismiss, and an ad above and below that changed while it rang.
  • Dismissing that one left us in the app, in front of a full-screen ad we had to close as well.
  • Its alarm editor repeats on days of the week only; there is no date to pick.
  • It asked us for a rating on our first day, before it had woken us a single morning.
  • Its alarm list carries a banner ad. One upgrade screen said 'One alarm is enough' and showed no price; another offered yearly, monthly and lifetime plans.

What Alarm Clock Planner does not do

  • Make you solve anything. Stopping is a slide, like the Clock app. If you need a harder stop, Alarmy is built for that; if you mostly need a second chance, backup alarms a few minutes apart, or one alarm with your own snooze length and snooze limit, usually do it.
  • Track your sleep, or ask for your microphone and camera. An Apple Watch worn to bed already records your sleep stages in the Health app, on its own.
  • Need a subscription. Up to 3 alarms are free, and $9.99 once unlocks the rest.
  • 0 ads, even in the free version.

Questions

Does Alarm Clock Planner have missions like Alarmy?

No. If you sleep through every alarm and need a puzzle or a photo to switch one off, Alarmy is the better choice. If you mostly need a second chance, two or three alarms a few minutes apart, or one alarm with a snooze length from 1 to 30 minutes and a limit of 1, 2 or 3 snoozes, often work better than a puzzle at 6 AM.

Can Alarmy set an alarm for a specific date?

No. Its alarm editor repeats on days of the week only, like the Clock app's, with no date to pick (checked in the app, October 2026).

Do both use Apple's native alarms?

Yes, both do on iOS 26. The difference is what comes after. Alarm Clock Planner's alarm is done when you stop it. When we dismissed Alarmy's native alarm, its own page rang a second time, with ads above and below, and then showed a full-screen ad.
