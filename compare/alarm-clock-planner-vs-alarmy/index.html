Alarmy alternative: Alarmy vs Alarm Clock Planner

These two apps solve different problems. Alarmy makes sure you get out of bed. Alarm Clock Planner makes sure the right alarm is waiting on the right day. Which one you want depends on which of those is your problem.*

Pick Alarmy if you sleep through ordinary alarms and need a task, like a math problem or a photo, before the alarm will stop.
Pick Alarm Clock Planner if you manage many alarms across dates and want a simple alarm with no ads, for $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Where Alarmy is stronger

Waking heavy sleepers

Missions, anti-snooze and power-off prevention are Alarmy's whole design. Alarm Clock Planner has nothing like them.

Sleep features

A sleep tracker, snore detection, sleep sounds and bedtime reminders. Alarm Clock Planner has none of these.

Reach

32 languages, older iPhones (iOS 17 and later) and about 250,000 US ratings.

Where Alarm Clock Planner is stronger

Alarms on dates

Pick any day on a calendar, with monthly, yearly and every-other-week repeats. Alarmy's listing does not describe alarms for a specific calendar date.

No ads

Alarmy's free version shows ad banners on its alarm list. Alarm Clock Planner has no ads, free or paid.

Organising many alarms

Tags, group on and off, bulk edit and a calendar view.

Price

$2.99 a year or $9.99 once. Alarmy's app offers yearly, monthly and lifetime plans: €59.99, €6.99 and €143.99 in its EU version; the US list shows $4.99 to $69.99.

About Alarm Clock Planner

An iPhone alarm clock built around the calendar, for alarms you plan ahead.

  • Alarms for any future date, picked on a calendar

What we saw using Alarmy

On 20 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • It asks for Apple's alarm permission (AlarmKit), and the alarm rang with the app closed.
  • The alarm rang on Alarmy's own full-screen screen, with one ad above it and another below while it was ringing.
  • Another time, the alarm played music with nothing on screen and raised the volume on its own; we had to open Notification Centre to find what was ringing.
  • Its alarm list carries a banner ad, and a rating prompt appeared after the first alarm.
  • One upgrade screen said 'One alarm is enough' and showed no price; another listed €59.99 a year, €6.99 a month or €143.99 once.

Alarm Clock Planner, same phone, same day:

  • With the app closed, the alarm rang as the iOS system alarm, with snooze and stop on the banner, the same as the Clock app.
  • Its upgrade screen, on the same EU store: €2.29 a year or €7.79 once, or carry on free with up to 3 alarms. No ads, no account.

What Alarm Clock Planner does not do

  • No wake-up missions or sleep tracking
  • No shared alarms

Pricing

Alarmy is free with ads. Its app offers yearly, monthly and lifetime plans with a 7-day free trial (€59.99, €6.99 and €143.99 in the EU version); the US App Store lists plans from $4.99 to $69.99. Alarm Clock Planner is free for up to 3 alarms; unlimited alarms cost $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Questions

Does Alarm Clock Planner have missions like Alarmy?

No. If you sleep through alarms and need a puzzle or a photo to switch one off, Alarmy is the better choice.

Can Alarmy set an alarm for a specific date?

Its App Store listing does not describe date-based alarms; it focuses on wake-up missions and sleep features.

Do both ring as system alarms?

Yes. On iOS 26 both ask for Apple's alarm permission (AlarmKit), so both can ring through Silent and Focus.

Free for up to 3 alarms. Rings on Silent. No account needed.
