Alarmy alternative: Alarmy vs Alarm Clock Planner

These two apps solve different problems. Alarmy makes sure you get out of bed. Alarm Clock Planner makes sure the right alarm is waiting on the right day. Which one you want depends on which of those is your problem.*

Pick Alarmy if you truly sleep through ordinary alarms and need a task, like a math problem or a photo, before the alarm will stop, and you can live with ads or a subscription.
Pick Alarm Clock Planner if you want an alarm that is easy to stop, backup alarms a few minutes apart instead of a puzzle, alarms planned by date, and 0 ads, for $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Where Alarmy is stronger

Waking heavy sleepers

Missions are Alarmy's whole design: solve math, shake the phone, do squats or photograph a spot in another room before the alarm stops, with options to block snoozing and switching the phone off. If nothing else gets you up, that works. The catch is the same thing that makes it work: at 6 AM you are solving a puzzle half asleep, and reviewers describe subscription prompts popping up at that moment too.

Sleep features

A sleep tracker, snore detection, sleep sounds and bedtime reminders, all in one app. If you wear an Apple Watch to bed, it already tracks your sleep into the Health app, so that part may be paying twice.

Reach

32 languages, about 250,000 US ratings, and iPhones back to iOS 17. On iPhones that cannot run iOS 26, though (the iPhone XS, XS Max and XR from 2018, and anything older), no app can use Apple's native alarms.

Where Alarm Clock Planner is stronger

Easy to stop, hard to sleep through

Instead of a puzzle, set two or three alarms a few minutes apart in one tag and switch them on or off together. Each one rings full screen as a native alarm, with Snooze and a slide to stop and nothing else around it. When Alarmy rang for us, its own full-screen page was a photo of a shouting cat, funny on the first morning, with an ad above the alarm and another below it.

You always know what is ringing

A native alarm puts its name on screen the moment it rings. In our test Alarmy once played music with nothing on screen and kept turning the volume up, and only Notification Centre told us where it came from.

Alarms on dates

Pick any day on a calendar, with monthly, yearly and every-other-week repeats and end dates. Alarmy's listing does not describe alarms for a specific calendar date.

Less to pay, 0 ads

Unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once, and there are 0 ads, even in the free version. Alarmy's free version shows ads, and its EU app asks €59.99 a year, €6.99 a month or €143.99 once; the US list shows $4.99 to $69.99.

What we saw using Alarmy

On 20 October 2026, on an iPhone 11 Pro Max with iOS 27.0.1, on an EU App Store account.†

  • It asks to use Apple's native alarms, and the alarm did go off with the app closed.
  • When it went off, it opened Alarmy's own full-screen page: a photo of a shouting cat, with one ad above the alarm and another below it.
  • Another time it played music with nothing on screen and kept turning the volume up. Nothing said what was ringing until we opened Notification Centre to find it.
  • Its alarm list carries a banner ad. One upgrade screen said 'One alarm is enough' and showed no price; another asked €59.99 a year, €6.99 a month or €143.99 once.

What Alarm Clock Planner does not do

  • Make you solve anything. Stopping is a slide, like the Clock app. If you need a harder stop, Alarmy is built for that; if you mostly need a second chance, backup alarms a few minutes apart and a snooze length you choose usually do it.
  • Track your sleep. If you want sleep data, an Apple Watch worn to bed records your sleep stages in the Health app, with no extra app listening.
  • Need a subscription. Up to 3 alarms are free, and $9.99 once unlocks the rest.
  • 0 ads, even in the free version.

Alarm Clock Planner's free version has 0 ads and holds up to 3 alarms at a time; unlimited alarms are $2.99 a year or $9.99 once.

Questions

Does Alarm Clock Planner have missions like Alarmy?

No. If you sleep through every alarm and need a puzzle or a photo to switch one off, Alarmy is the better choice. If you mostly need a second chance, two or three alarms a few minutes apart, switched on and off together, often work better than a puzzle at 6 AM.

Can Alarmy set an alarm for a specific date?

Its App Store listing does not describe date-based alarms; it focuses on wake-up missions and sleep features.

Do both use Apple's native alarms?

Yes, on iOS 26 both ask for them, so both can ring through Silent and Focus. The difference is what you see: Alarm Clock Planner shows the alarm full screen like the Clock app, while Alarmy rang on its own page with ads in our test, and once with nothing on screen at all.
