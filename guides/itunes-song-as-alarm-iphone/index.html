How to Use a Purchased iTunes Song as Your iPhone Alarm

You bought that song years ago, and you still like it. It can wake you up tomorrow, and it takes about five minutes to set up.

Quick answer: Songs bought on the iTunes Store since 2009 come without copy protection, so the song is a file you own. Download it in the Music app on your Mac, save it to iCloud Drive or AirDrop it to your iPhone, then in Alarm Clock Planner tap Add custom sound and pick it from Files. Up to 30 seconds of it play when the alarm rings, so trim it first if the best part comes later. Songs from an Apple Music subscription are protected and cannot be used.

Will your song work?

It depends on where the song came from:

  • Bought on the iTunes Store since April 2009: yes. These are iTunes Plus files, 256 kbps AAC with no copy protection. The file ends in .m4a.
  • Bought before 2009: maybe. Older purchases were sold with copy protection and end in .m4p. Those cannot be imported.
  • Added from an Apple Music subscription: no. Those songs are protected so they only play inside the Music app, even after you download them.
  • Ripped from a CD, or bought as an MP3 elsewhere: yes. Any plain audio file works the same way.

Step 1: Get the song file on your Mac

  1. Open the Music app. If the song is not on this Mac yet, find it in your library or under Account > Purchased, and download it.
  2. Click Songs in the sidebar and select the song.
  3. Choose File > Show in Finder. The .m4a file is now selected in a Finder window.

On a Windows PC the same purchase downloads in the Apple Music app or iTunes, and you can move it to your iPhone through iCloud Drive on icloud.com.

Step 2: Move it to your iPhone

Either way works:

  • iCloud Drive: drag the file into iCloud Drive in Finder. A moment later it appears in the Files app on your iPhone.
  • AirDrop: right-click the file, choose Share > AirDrop, and pick your iPhone. When the iPhone asks where to open it, choose Files and save it somewhere you will find again.

Step 3: Start it at the chorus (optional)

An alarm plays up to 30 seconds. Alarm Clock Planner skips any silence at the start and keeps the first 30 seconds of sound, so a song with a long intro wakes you to the intro. To start somewhere else, trim it on your Mac first:

  1. Open the song in QuickTime Player.
  2. Choose Edit > Trim and drag the yellow handles around the part you want.
  3. Choose File > Export As > Audio Only, then move that file to your iPhone instead.

Step 4: Set it as your alarm sound

  1. In Alarm Clock Planner, create or edit an alarm and open the sound list.
  2. Tap Add custom sound and pick the file in Files.
  3. A local copy is saved in the app, so the alarm never depends on streaming or on the file staying where you put it.
  4. The song now sits next to the built-in tones. Use it for one alarm, a whole group, or as your default.
If the app says it cannot use the file, the song is protected: an Apple Music song or an older .m4p purchase. A song bought since 2009 imports without a problem.

Why not just pick the song in the Clock app?

The built-in Clock app can play a song from your music library, but that path has a long record of ringing with the default tone instead, usually because the song was not stored on the phone when the alarm went off. Importing the file gives the alarm its own copy, so it plays the song every time. More on that in the custom alarm sound guide.

Can you do it on the iPhone alone?

Not from the Music app: it does not hand purchased songs to other apps as files, so the file has to come from a computer once. If the song already sits in Files on your iPhone, for example because you saved it there earlier, skip straight to step 4.
