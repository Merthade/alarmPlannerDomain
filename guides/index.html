iPhone Alarm Guides

Straight answers to the alarm questions the built-in Clock app raises, and how Alarm Clock Planner handles each one. No fluff, verified against current iOS behavior.

Most of these guides exist because of the same underlying design decision: an alarm in the iPhone Clock app is a time, plus optionally a set of weekdays, and nothing else. No date, no interval, no way to skip a single morning. That one constraint is where almost every question below comes from, and it has not changed in years.

Each guide states what the built-in Clock app can and cannot do first, then the workarounds worth knowing, then how Alarm Clock Planner handles it. Where the honest answer is that something is not possible, or that a workaround is bad, the guide says that instead of selling around it.

Dates and one-off alarms

The gap people hit first: an alarm in the built-in Clock app is a time plus, optionally, days of the week. There is no date field anywhere in it. Set an alarm for 05:30 and it rings at the next 05:30, which is today or tomorrow, never the specific day you had in mind. Repeating alarms

The Clock app repeats on days of the week and nothing else, so anything measured in months, years, or intervals cannot be expressed at all. The usual workaround, the Reminders app, does support those repeats but delivers a notification rather than an alarm, which is a meaningful difference at 6 AM. Common questions

Can the iPhone Clock app set an alarm for a specific date?

No. An alarm there is a time plus, optionally, days of the week, and as of iOS 26 there is still no date field. For a date inside the next 7 days you can set the matching weekday and delete the alarm after it fires. Beyond that you need Shortcuts or an alarm app built around dates. Read the guide

Do iPhone alarms ring on Silent mode?

Yes. Alarms are exempt from the Silent switch, and from Do Not Disturb and Focus modes. This is one of the few places iOS deliberately overrides your own settings, and it is why an alarm is not the same thing as a notification. Read the guide

What is the difference between an alarm and a reminder?

A reminder is a notification: it plays a short tone at notification volume, respects Silent and Focus, and stops by itself. An alarm interrupts. For an errand at 3 PM the difference does not matter. For medication or a 4:30 start it is the whole point. Read the guide

Can you set a monthly or yearly alarm on iPhone?

Not in the Clock app, whose only repeat unit is the weekday. The Reminders app can repeat monthly and yearly but sends a notification rather than an alarm. A dedicated alarm app can do monthly, yearly, and every-N intervals with a real alarm at the end of them. Read the guide

Can you skip one occurrence of a repeating alarm?

The Clock app has no skip option, so the only route is switching the alarm off and remembering to switch it back on. That forgotten second step is the usual cause of oversleeping the day after a day off. Read the guide

Is there an alarm widget for iPhone?

Not from Apple. The built-in Clock app ships no widget, so there is nothing to add from the stock alarm. Third-party alarm apps can provide Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets showing your next alarm. Read the guide
